The Collision Horizon

AI capability compounds roughly geometrically. The marketing ecosystem adapts the way large organisations always have: in budget cycles, reorganisations and multi-year transformation plans. Plot both on one axis and they diverge. The distance between them, the collision horizon, is where every theme below plays out.

The gap is not uniform. It opens at different speeds by task, role, sector, company size and risk tolerance, and handing teams AI seats moves the capability curve inside the building without moving the adaptation curve at all.

And "exposure" is not "automation" is not "job loss." Treat every number here as a signpost, not a guarantee.

Method & Limitations

The hypotheses come from a working white paper synthesising the AMA Chicago AI Marketing Forum discussion of 30 September 2026, a working forecast exercise rather than a formal research study, in which participants contributed observations from their professional experience across the marketing ecosystem. The implications and signals are the working group's own extrapolation of those hypotheses, informed by published research.

Read everything here as scenarios, discussion-derived themes and hypotheses: not statistically representative findings, and not official predictions of the American Marketing Association. Where external research is cited, evaluate it on its own methodology.
